## 摘要

The Cullinan kimberlite (formerly Premier) provides a unique opportunity to investigate the composition, stratigraphy, and metasomatic evolution of the subcontinental lithospheric mantle beneath the Kaapvaal craton. This study is based on mantle indicator minerals and integrates petrography, major-element geochemistry (EPMA), and trace-element systematics (LA-ICP-MS) with thermobarometry to constrain the pressure–temperature conditions of the lithospheric mantle sampled by four kimberlite facies: Blue, Black Transitional, Pale Piebald, and Fawn. Garnet and clinopyroxene xenocrysts record derivation from both depleted harzburgitic and more fertile lherzolitic mantle assemblages, consistent with sampling of a vertically stratified lithospheric mantle column. Thermobarometric estimates define equilibrium pressures extending into the diamond stability field, indicating that the Cullinan magmas sampled mantle domains capable of preserving diamond. Trace-element systematics reveal multiple stages of mantle metasomatism. Garnet populations display variable Ti, Zr, and REE signatures that record early carbonatitic enrichment followed by later silicate-melt and phlogopite-bearing metasomatism. In contrast, the Fawn kimberlite contains Si-deficient hydro-andradite with fracture-controlled replacement textures, indicative of low-temperature (<350 °C) post-emplacement hydrothermal alteration. Serpentinisation of olivine macrocrysts is pervasive across all facies, further demonstrating late-stage fluid circulation during cooling. Taken together, the mineralogical and geochemical evidence indicates that the Cullinan kimberlite sampled a modified, Proterozoic-reworked lithospheric mantle that retains residual Archean thermal structure and stability. These findings refine the mantle sampling profile and metasomatic history of the Cullinan system and have broader implications for diamond preservation and kimberlite melt evolution.
