## 摘要

Abstract Earth’s deep carbon-cycle has been active since subduction of carbon-rich oceanic crust began in the Paleoproterozoic. Petrological evidence for carbonatite melts suggests they are a significant part of the mantle carbon reservoir and act as a highly efficient, volatile-rich transporting agent that dissolve, mobilise, and transfer rare earth and sulphur-bearing elements from deep mantle into the crust at craton margins. However, direct geophysical imaging of carbonatite melts in the mantle are ambiguous as electrical resistivity and shear-wave velocity anomalies can also be ascribed to hydrous silicates in solid or small melt fractions. In this paper we use long-period MT data to image an electrically anisotropic and conductive (~1 Ohm.m) lower lithosphere (180 km depth) beneath the Archean core of the Gawler Craton in southern Australia. We interpret these features as metasomatically modified and channelised structures, potentially associated with carbon-bearing melts, where small fractions (~0.05 vol%) of carbonatite melt account for observed low resistivity. Additionally, low resistivity (<100 Ohm.m) regions are imaged at Moho depths in an arcuate belt around the Archean Craton, coincident with a shear-wave velocity decrease. Such low resistivity regions are interpreted to be the signature of carbon exsolved as graphite from upward migrating deep-mantle carbonatite melts.
